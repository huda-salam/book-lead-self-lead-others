# Leader Mindset Reframe v0.1

Status: Approved architectural direction for the next drafting cycle.

## 1. Core positioning
This book is fundamentally a book about **leader mindset and leadership capability**.
It is not a response-management book.
The ability to regulate and choose a response is one important leadership capability because leaders repeatedly face pressure, ambiguity, disagreement, incomplete information, competing interests, and consequences. Response capability is therefore treated as a **component of leadership**, not the organizing identity of the book.
### Working thesis
> **Leadership begins with how a person sees, thinks, chooses, acts, relates, and learns when reality is more complicated than the first story in their head.**
The book trains the reader to see beyond the immediate stimulus; manage self without becoming self-absorbed; read situations and people without pretending to know what cannot be known; make decisions under uncertainty and trade-offs; communicate while preserving clarity and dignity; handle conflict, power, interests, and organizational reality; take ownership without taking responsibility for everything; develop other people; think beyond first-order effects; review outcomes; and turn principles into repeatable practice.

## 2. Leader Mindset as the organizing model
Primary operating loop:
**SEE → UNDERSTAND → DECIDE → ACT → LEARN**
Expanded mechanism:
**Situation → Read Self + Read Situation + Read Others → Regulate + Reframe → Decide → Communicate / Act → Observe Consequences → Review → Learn / Update**
This is a **book synthesis / working model**, not a validated psychological taxonomy.
### Important implication
Response belongs primarily to the **Decide → Communicate / Act** transition, supported by self-regulation and situational reading.
Do not frame the whole book as stimulus → response control.
Frame it as **reality → interpretation → judgment → choice → action → consequence → learning**.

## 4. Repositioning of response capability
Response capability remains important but must not dominate the reader-facing identity.
Use: memilih respons rather than mengendalikan respons; merespons sebagai leader rather than mengelola reaksi; response capability may appear as a capability label; avoid making response the explanation for every leadership problem.
### Guardrail
A leader is not defined by always responding calmly, positively, or softly.
A leader may need to challenge, refuse, escalate, decide quickly, deliver bad news, enforce a boundary, change a plan, admit uncertainty, or stop an unsafe action.
The leadership question is not whether the response was pleasant. It is whether the response was proportionate to the situation, grounded in available evidence, aligned with legitimate objectives and constraints, and followed by ownership and learning.
This is a book-level synthesis, not a validated leadership scale.

## 5. Tone and reader promise
The reader should feel that the book is training **judgment and leadership capability**, not merely self-improvement.
Prefer questions such as: apa yang perlu Anda lihat? apa yang belum Anda ketahui? apa yang sedang dipertaruhkan? pilihan apa yang tersedia? apa konsekuensi tingkat kedua? siapa yang terdampak? apa yang menjadi tanggung jawab Anda? kapan Anda perlu mengubah metode? apa yang akan Anda review setelah bertindak?
Avoid overusing jangan emosional, selalu tenang, berpikir positif, kontrol diri, atau jadilah orang baik. These may appear as local advice only when the mechanism and boundary are explicit.

## 7. Chapter-opening contract
From Chapter 1 onward, each chapter should answer early:
1. Why this matters to a leader
2. What capability is being trained
3. What mechanism explains the capability
4. Where the capability can fail or be misapplied
5. What the reader should practice
A chapter may begin with an ordinary-life situation, but it should connect the situation to leadership capability quickly.

## 8. Leadership capability test
Before finalizing a chapter, ask:
- What leadership capability does this chapter strengthen?
- What leadership decision becomes better informed because of it?
- What can go wrong if the concept is overapplied?
- What evidence supports the mechanism?
- What belongs to another chapter?
- Can a reader practice the capability rather than merely agree with it?
If the answer is mainly this helps you feel better about yourself, the chapter needs reframing.

## 9. Visual/editorial system
The visual system should reinforce epistemic and practical distinctions rather than decorate the page.
Use INFO for context and definitions; TIP for practical technique; IMPORTANT for a principle that materially changes application; CAUTION for likely misuse or trade-off; WARNING for meaningful organizational/interpersonal risk; DANGER for hard boundaries; NOTE or GREEN NOTE for concise synthesis; RED NOTE for red flags or anti-patterns; DETAILS for optional depth.
Use custom Indonesian titles consistently. These are presentation semantics, not claims about evidence strength.
Suggested visual vocabulary: Info Konteks; Tip Praktik; Important Prinsip; Caution Hati-hati; Warning Risiko; Danger Batas Keras; Success Green Note; Danger Red Note; Details Baca Lebih Dalam.
The distinction between green and red notes is editorial: green means reinforce/carry forward; red means stop/question/verify. Do not use color to imply that a claim is scientifically proven or disproven.

## 11. Density rule
Visual emphasis must be selective. Ordinary prose remains ordinary prose. Use one strong synthesis callout per major section where useful; warnings only when there is a real misuse or consequence; details blocks for optional depth; tables for comparisons and decision aids; short numbered procedures for operational methods.
Avoid turning every paragraph into a colored card.

## 12. Canonical rule
The book's visual language must support the same epistemic discipline as Book OS:
**evidence ≠ interpretation ≠ synthesis ≠ recommendation.**
A colored box does not upgrade the truth status of its contents.

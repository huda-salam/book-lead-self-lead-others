## Drafting constraints

1. Do not turn conceptual synthesis into empirical fact.
2. Do not strengthen a source claim beyond its population, design, context, or evidence quality.
3. Keep Book says / Evidence shows / Inference / Practice distinguishable.
4. Preserve canonical ownership; do not redefine another chapter's core concept silently.
5. Every empirical or causal claim must have a source record.
6. Cases must distinguish observable facts, interpretation, mechanism hypothesis, evidence, and transfer boundary.
7. Religious examples must preserve source hierarchy and distinguish text from interpretation and historical report.
8. Public-figure/business examples must control for survivorship, halo, selection, and retrospective storytelling.
9. Practical heuristics must be labeled as heuristics when they are not validated interventions.
10. Avoid moral diagnosis from isolated behavior.
11. Persistence must never become “never stop.”
12. Consistency must never become “never change method.”
13. Discipline must never become a measure of moral worth.
14. Goal discipline means commitment to the goal/problem, not attachment to the solution.
15. Literacy discipline requires proportional verification, not endless research.

## Chapter-level gate

Before a chapter is considered draft-complete:
- canonical owner is explicit;
- evidence claims are sourced;
- mechanism is explained;
- boundary is visible;
- at least one counterexample or failure mode is included;
- practical exercise is connected to the mechanism;
- retrieval entry points are defined;
- terminology collisions are checked;
- chapter does not duplicate another canonical owner.

## Status distinction

Drafting unlocked does not mean the book is finished or empirically validated. Each chapter remains subject to structural, conceptual, evidence, terminology, cross-chapter, red-team, retrieval, and editorial QA.

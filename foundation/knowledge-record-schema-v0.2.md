## 2. Required distinctions

### Discipline vs consistency
- Discipline = capability/process of executing a chosen standard.
- Consistency = observed/repeated pattern of execution over time.
- A person can have a strong intention to be disciplined without yet demonstrating consistency.
- Consistency should not be measured only by frequency; quality, context, and sustainability matter.

### Persistence vs rigidity
- Persistence = continue pursuing a valid goal while adapting when warranted.
- Rigidity = continue the same approach despite evidence that it should be reconsidered.
- “Tidak menyerah” is not sufficient evidence of persistence quality.

### Goal discipline vs solution attachment
- Goal discipline preserves the objective while allowing method changes.
- Solution attachment preserves a preferred method even when the objective is no longer served.
- Sunk-cost reasoning, ego investment, identity, or fear of admitting error can be hypotheses to examine, not assumed causes.

### Literacy discipline vs skepticism/cynicism
- Literacy discipline asks what is known, how well it is known, what remains uncertain, and what evidence would change the conclusion.
- Skepticism can be useful; cynicism is not a substitute for evidence.
- Over-analysis and endless source checking can themselves become avoidance; proportionality remains necessary.

## 3. Existing schema remains canonical

All fields and record types from v0.1 remain valid. The AI contract must preserve the new distinctions and must not translate “discipline” into moral blame when a failure may instead reflect unrealistic commitment, changed constraints, poor method, or an invalid goal.

## 4. Evidence status for new concepts

Until targeted research is finalized:
- definitions above = conceptual/architectural definitions;
- empirical claims about behavior change, goal attainment, persistence, or intervention effectiveness remain research-required;
- the user's described pattern is a design input/case hypothesis, not population evidence.

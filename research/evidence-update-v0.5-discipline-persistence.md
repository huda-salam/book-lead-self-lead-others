# Evidence Update v0.5 — Discipline, Consistency, Persistence, Goal Adjustment

> Status: research update for chapter architecture. This strengthens the evidence basis for the new discipline/persistence integration but does not justify treating the book's personal operating system as an empirically validated intervention.

## 1. Self-control and goal-aligned action

Duckworth et al. (2019), Annual Review of Psychology, define self-control in terms of aligning thoughts, feelings, and actions with enduringly valued goals when more immediately attractive alternatives compete with those goals. Their review reports longitudinal associations between self-control and academic attainment, while also emphasizing that self-control is not the only relevant factor. citeturn0search0

Design implication: SELF.DISCIPLINE can legitimately be framed as goal-aligned execution under competing short-term attractions, but the book should not reduce achievement to discipline alone.

Boundary: this evidence is primarily about self-control and academic achievement, not proof that a particular workplace discipline routine will produce goal attainment.

## 2. Persistence is not the same as never stopping

Brandstätter & Bernecker (2022), Annual Review of Psychology, review persistence and timely goal disengagement as complementary parts of self-regulation. The review explicitly treats both persistence and disengagement as relevant to well-being and performance and identifies unresolved questions. citeturn0search1turn0search35

Design implication: the book should teach two capabilities together:
- continue when the goal remains valid and the path remains viable or can be adapted;
- disengage or revise when evidence, constraints, or goal validity justify doing so.

This directly supports the distinction: persistence is not stubbornness and stopping is not automatic failure.

## 3. Self-regulation is dynamic

Neal, Ballard & Vancouver (2017), Annual Review of Organizational Psychology and Organizational Behavior, describe self-regulation as a dynamic process involving management of competing demands, task/goal selection, timing, and adjustments to goals. citeturn0search2

Recent review work on cognitive control likewise emphasizes selecting behavior according to context and monitoring ongoing behavior to adjust it. citeturn0search12

Design implication: consistency in this book should not mean mechanically repeating identical behavior. A better formulation is:
> stable commitment where the reason remains valid; adaptive execution where circumstances or evidence change.

## 4. Habits and behavior maintenance

Verplanken & Orbell (2022), Annual Review of Psychology, emphasize that habits can become boundary conditions for behavior-change interventions and examine how repeated behavior, context, planning, and habit architecture interact with goal pursuit. citeturn0search3

Wood & Rünger (2016), Annual Review of Psychology, describe habits as repeated responses in recurring contexts that interact with deliberate goal pursuit. citeturn0search7

Design implication: the book should not rely on motivation as the main maintenance mechanism. It should examine cues, context, friction, repeated behavior, and recovery after lapses.

Boundary: habit research does not establish that every desired behavior becomes automatic or that a simple routine will reliably produce long-term outcomes.

## 5. Implementation intentions / if-then planning

A recent Annual Review of Psychology review on planning reports positive effects associated with if-then planning on thinking, feeling, and acting, while noting moderators involving people, goals, and plan characteristics. citeturn0search13

Design implication: Chapter 44 can use implementation-intention-style structures such as:
- “Jika saya mulai menunda, maka saya mengerjakan langkah terkecil yang sudah ditentukan.”
- “Jika saya melewatkan satu hari, maka saya kembali pada sesi berikutnya; saya tidak menunggu minggu depan.”
- “Jika evidence menunjukkan metode tidak bekerja, maka saya review metode tanpa otomatis membuang goal.”

Boundary: these are practice translations, not guarantees of behavior change.

## 6. Culture and context

An APA summary of recent research reports that an intervention centered on implementation intentions produced different results when adapted to a more interdependent cultural context, with the culturally tailored intervention showing significant effects at one year in that study. citeturn0search10

Design implication: do not present self-discipline techniques developed in one cultural context as universally optimal. The book is Indonesian and should test examples against Indonesian workplace, family, and organizational contexts.

## 7. Core evidence-backed architecture

The research supports the architecture-level proposition that long-term goal pursuit involves more than initial motivation:

Goal value → competing impulses/demands → self-regulation → repeated action → monitoring → adjustment → persistence or disengagement

The evidence does not establish a universal formula for success, nor does it prove that the book's specific exercises will produce long-term goal attainment.

## 8. Revised conceptual rule

The book should therefore teach:

> Jangan ukur disiplin hanya dari seberapa keras Anda memulai. Ukur dari kemampuan menjaga arah, menjalankan langkah yang realistis, membaca feedback, memulihkan lapse, dan mengubah metode ketika evidence menuntutnya.

This is a pedagogical synthesis, not a direct quotation or causal finding from any single study.

## 9. Research gaps before drafting

Still required:
- stronger evidence on workplace/general adult goal pursuit;
- implementation intentions and behavior-change effect sizes in relevant populations;
- persistence versus goal disengagement under changing constraints;
- goal adjustment and reengagement;
- habit formation/maintenance and lapse recovery;
- interaction between self-regulation, emotion regulation, and realistic commitment;
- evidence relevant to Indonesian/collectivist organizational contexts where available.

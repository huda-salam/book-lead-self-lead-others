## Findings

1. **Leader capability framing — PASS**  
   Problem framing is positioned as a core leadership capability rather than a technical analysis exercise alone.

2. **Boundary with Chapter 10 — PASS**  
   Listening remains Chapter 10; Chapter 21 converts what is heard into a problem representation.

3. **Boundary with Chapter 22 — PASS**  
   Hypothesis testing and resistance to premature solutions remain Chapter 22.

4. **Boundary with Chapter 23 — PASS**  
   Causal/root-cause analysis remains Chapter 23.

5. **Mechanism — PASS**  
   The chapter distinguishes complaint, symptom, problem, cause, solution, decision, and trade-off.

6. **Evidence posture — PASS WITH PROVENANCE CONDITION**  
   Problem framing is connected to literature on problem representation and judgment/decision making, without claiming that the book's five-step framework is a validated taxonomy.

7. **Experience versus evidence — PASS**  
   Complaints are treated as potentially valuable experience data while interpretations remain subject to testing.

8. **Anti-solution bias — PASS**  
   The chapter explicitly warns against embedding a preferred solution into the problem statement.

9. **Structural diagnosis — PASS**  
   Problems may involve process, capacity, incentives, authority, or system design rather than character.

10. **Trade-off boundary — PASS**  
    Some apparent problems are genuine trade-offs or unresolved choices rather than conditions that can simply be eliminated.

11. **Anti-analysis-paralysis — PASS**  
    `Minimum sufficient framing` preserves movement when the problem is clear enough for the next step.

12. **Practical framework — PASS**  
    `Listen → Translate → Bound → Test → Frame` is explicitly labeled as a book synthesis, not a validated problem-framing taxonomy.

13. **Ethics/accountability boundary — PASS**  
    Problem framing cannot be used to erase misconduct, safety issues, or ethical problems.

14. **Red-team — PASS WITH MINOR PROVENANCE CONDITION**  
    Final publication requires bibliography verification for framing/problem-representation literature and careful distinction between symptoms, causes, and problem statements.

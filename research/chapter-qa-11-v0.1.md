# Chapter 11 QA — v0.1

## QA result

**Overall: PASS WITH REVISIONS**

Chapter 11 establishes COMM.RESPECT as the practice of maintaining human dignity while allowing substantive disagreement.

## 1. Conceptual QA — PASS

The chapter clearly distinguishes:

- respect from agreement;
- disagreement from disrespect;
- person/dignity from claim validity;
- accountability from humiliation;
- hierarchy from truth;
- politeness from genuine respect;
- respect from permissiveness.

The core working rule is:

> **Hormati manusianya. Periksa gagasannya. Nilai tindakannya.**

This is a book synthesis, not a validated formal model.

## 2. Cultural-context QA — PASS WITH REVISIONS

The chapter incorporates Indonesian and Javanese cultural context without making cultural generalizations universal.

The concepts:

- *unggah-ungguh*;
- *andhap asor*;
- *tepa selira*;
- *ajining diri saka lathi*

are explicitly framed as cultural/philosophical lenses rather than empirical psychological constructs.

Required final editorial check:
- verify wording/provenance of each Javanese expression against an appropriate cultural/literary source before publication;
- avoid implying that all Javanese people interpret these concepts identically;
- avoid turning cultural values into prescriptions that suppress legitimate disagreement.

## 3. Respect and disagreement — PASS

The chapter maintains the canonical distinction:

> respect ≠ agreement.

It also preserves:

> disagreement ≠ disrespect.

This is central to COMM.RESPECT and should remain distinct from listening in Chapter 10.

## 4. Status/hierarchy — PASS WITH REVISIONS

The chapter appropriately treats hierarchy as relevant to:
- authority;
- responsibility;
- information flow;
- perceived risk of disagreement.

It does not claim that hierarchy always suppresses voice.

Final evidence pass should ensure any empirical hierarchy/status statement is supported by a source and kept bounded.

## 5. Accountability boundary — PASS

The chapter correctly states that respect does not require tolerating:
- abuse;
- intimidation;
- rule violations;
- misuse of authority;
- harmful behavior.

The behavior-based framing should remain consistent with Chapter 7.

## 6. Cultural balance — PASS

The chapter avoids two opposite errors:

### Error A
“Jawa harus selalu halus, jadi jangan frontal.”

### Error B
“Budaya tidak penting; yang penting bicara sekeras mungkin.”

The intended position is:

> **Adab mengatur cara; evidence and responsibility govern substance.**

This is a book synthesis and should be labeled as such where necessary.

## 8. Red-team

### Does respect become agreement?
No.

### Does respect become passivity?
No.

### Does Javanese etiquette become a reason to hide problems?
Explicitly no.

### Does “tepa selira” become “never make anyone uncomfortable”?
No.

### Does hierarchy become proof of truth?
No.

### Does criticism become character attack?
The chapter explicitly redirects toward behavior, impact, standards, and action.

### Does cultural framing become essentialism?
Low risk; the chapter uses contextual language and explicitly avoids universalizing. Final source/provenance pass remains required.

### Does politeness become manipulation?
The section on surface politeness vs actual respect addresses this.

## 9. Practice QA — PASS

The “Disagree Without Diminishing” drill directly operationalizes the canonical concept.

The practice separates:
- position;
- other person's position;
- common ground;
- difference;
- evidence;
- delivery;
- revision.

It should remain identified as a book learning tool, not a validated intervention.

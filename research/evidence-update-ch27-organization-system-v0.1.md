# Evidence Update — Chapter 27: Organisasi Bukan Hanya Struktur Resmi

## Evidence focus

Chapter 27 introduces the organization as a social and operating system, not merely an organigram. The chapter must distinguish formal structure from informal relationships, routines, information flows, incentives, norms, and decision processes.

### 1. Formal structure is necessary but incomplete

An organizational chart describes formal authority and reporting relationships. It does not by itself describe how information travels, how work actually gets coordinated, where informal influence sits, or how norms shape behavior.

### 2. Organizations are systems of interdependent elements

Organizational behavior research commonly examines interactions among structure, people, tasks, technology, environment, incentives, and processes. The chapter should avoid presenting one universal organizational model.

### 3. Informal organization matters

Informal networks can influence information exchange, coordination, trust, access, and implementation. Informal influence is not automatically bad or political manipulation.

### 4. Routines shape behavior

Repeated routines can stabilize coordination and reduce cognitive/coordination costs, but can also persist after the conditions that created them have changed.

### 5. Information flows are part of organizational design

A leader should ask not only “who reports to whom?” but also:
- who knows what;
- who needs what information;
- where information stops;
- who can authorize action;
- and where handoffs occur.

### 6. Local optimization can create system problems

A unit can optimize its own target while making the overall system worse. Cross-functional dependencies therefore matter for leadership decisions.

### 7. Organizational behavior is contextual

The chapter should avoid universal claims such as “bureaucracy is bad” or “informal networks are good.” Effects depend on task, governance, incentives, culture, power, and context.

## Editorial implications

- Teach the reader to map the organization beyond the org chart.
- Separate formal authority from informal influence.
- Treat norms and routines as mechanisms, not personality traits.
- Identify information bottlenecks and handoffs.
- Look for local incentives that produce system-level side effects.
- Avoid romanticizing informal networks.
- Avoid reducing organizational problems to individual attitude.
- Preserve governance and accountability boundaries.

## Proposed book synthesis

**Structure → Roles → Workflows → Information → Incentives → Norms → Networks → Decisions → Feedback**

This is a book working model, not a validated organizational taxonomy.

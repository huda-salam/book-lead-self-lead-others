# Final Evidence Review v0.1 — Pre-Drafting Gate

## Decision

**Evidence gate: PASS WITH CONDITIONS.**

The architecture has enough evidence for the book's central pedagogical claims, but not every chapter is ready for unrestricted empirical/causal prose. Research-required chapters must keep their claims bounded until their chapter-specific source packets are completed.

## Evidence backbone currently sufficient

### Self-regulation, discipline, persistence, and adjustment
- Duckworth et al. (2019), Annual Review of Psychology: self-control is discussed as alignment with enduring valued goals despite attractive alternatives.
- Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006), meta-analysis of 94 independent tests: implementation intentions showed a positive effect on goal attainment in the reviewed literature.
- Brandstätter & Bernecker (2022), Annual Review of Psychology: persistence and timely disengagement are complementary self-regulation components.
- 2025 meta-analytic review of goal adjustment: disengagement, reengagement, and striving flexibility have distinct antecedents/outcomes; evidence quality has important limitations.
- Barlow, Wrosch & McGrath (2020): goal disengagement/reengagement capacities show associations with quality-of-life indicators.
- 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis of habit formation: maintenance varies by behavior/person; evidence quality limitations prevent universal timing claims.
- Roordink et al. (2023): maintenance/lapse in adult health behaviors is difficult and has multiple predictors.
- Neal, Ballard & Vancouver (2017): self-regulation is dynamic and includes competing demands, goal/task selection, sequencing, and adjustment.

Editorial consequence: the book may teach disciplined execution, persistence, recovery, and adaptation as a **pedagogical synthesis**, but must not claim that discipline guarantees success or that one recovery protocol works universally.

### Listening, psychological safety, humility
Existing evidence map contains:
- Kluger et al. (2024) listening meta-analysis.
- Frazier et al. (2017) psychological safety meta-analysis.
- Chandler et al. (2023) meta-analysis of humble leadership, with construct/measurement limitations.
- Longmire & Harrison (2018) on perspective-taking and empathic concern with contextual contingencies.

Editorial consequence: use mechanism + boundary language; avoid treating humility, listening, psychological safety, or perspective-taking as universally beneficial or interchangeable constructs.

### Humor
A workplace-leadership scoping review found humor to be multidimensional, with outcomes varying by humor style, leadership relationship, appropriateness, and cultural context. Affiliative/self-enhancing and aggressive/self-defeating styles should not be collapsed into one “humor” effect. A 2026 review of leader aggressive humor further confirms that the topic has mixed and context-dependent outcomes.

Sources:
- Huo et al./workplace leadership humor scoping review, 2021, PubMed PMID 34385944.
- Mishra & Dhar, AOM Proceedings 2026, DOI 10.5465/AMPROC.2026.20407.

Editorial consequence: Chapter 13 should remain a risk-management chapter, not a formula for “safe jokes.”

### Relationship repair
A systematic review/meta-analysis of apology and trust repair reported a positive average association/effect with moderators; later workplace research on leader apologies examined trust and forgiveness mechanisms. This supports teaching apology as one possible repair mechanism, not as a guarantee of restored trust.

Sources:
- Yuan, Dong & Li (2017), Advances in Psychological Science, DOI 10.3724/SP.J.1042.2017.01103.
- 2024 European Journal of Work and Organizational Psychology study on leader apologies, DOI 10.1080/1359432X.2024.2319903.

Editorial consequence: Chapter 20 must distinguish apology, forgiveness, trust repair, and restored performance.

### Informal organization and networks
Research reviewed for the architecture supports treating organizations as more than formal charts: informal networks can provide information/resources and can interact with formal structures and performance. Evidence is heterogeneous and context-dependent.

Sources include organizational-network literature reviewed in the research packet, including Soltis et al. (2023) and work on formal/informal network alignment.

Editorial consequence: Chapter 27 may teach network awareness, but should not imply that informal influence is inherently good, bad, or equivalent to political manipulation.

### Workplace aggression / relational harm
A 2022/2023 meta-analysis of leadership and workplace aggression covered 165 samples and 115,190 participants and found associations between leadership styles and workplace aggression, with important moderators. This supports treating relational harm as a legitimate organizational mechanism while avoiding simplistic leader-style causality.

Source:
- Cao et al., Journal of Business Ethics, 2023, DOI 10.1007/s10551-022-05184-0.

Editorial consequence: Chapters 18–20 and 30 should distinguish observable behavior, interpretation, intent, and organizational conditions.

These chapters are **not deleted or structurally blocked**; their drafting must simply avoid stronger claims than their evidence supports.

## Evidence language rule

Use:
- “Evidence suggests…”
- “Research reviewed here indicates…”
- “This chapter proposes a practical framework…”
- “This is a working heuristic, not a validated universal rule.”

Avoid:
- “Research proves…”
- “People always…”
- “If you are disciplined, you will succeed.”
- “This method works for everyone.”
- “This behavior proves the person is manipulative/lazy/weak.”

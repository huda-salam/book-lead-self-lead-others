# Evidence Update — Chapter 36: Delegasi Bukan Memindahkan Beban

## Evidence focus

Delegation is a management mechanism for allocating work and decision authority while retaining appropriate accountability, support, and review. Effective delegation is not simply transferring tasks away from the leader.

### Core evidence posture

- Delegation involves transferring responsibility for specified work and, where appropriate, decision authority.
- Delegation is constrained by capability, authority, information, risk, and task interdependence.
- Clear expectations and outcome definitions improve coordination.
- Micromanagement and abandonment are opposite failure modes.
- Effective delegation requires appropriate autonomy plus visibility.
- Delegation can develop capability when paired with support, feedback, and increasing ownership.
- Accountability should remain explicit: the person doing the work needs clear responsibility, while the leader retains responsibility for the management system and decisions within the leader's authority.
- Not every task should be delegated; high-risk, confidential, authority-bound, or genuinely leader-specific decisions may require leader involvement.
- Delegation should be reviewed and adjusted as evidence changes.

## Editorial implications

1. Delegate outcomes and decision boundaries, not merely chores.
2. Specify authority: what can the delegate decide without asking?
3. Specify constraints: budget, policy, risk, quality, deadline.
4. Specify escalation triggers.
5. Match delegation level to capability and stakes.
6. Establish checkpoints without requiring approval for every minor step.
7. Do not take work back at the first imperfect result.
8. Do not leave someone alone after assigning responsibility.
9. Give credit accurately.
10. Use delegation to reduce bottlenecks and build organizational capacity, not merely to offload unpleasant work.

## Proposed book synthesis

**Select → Define Outcome → Assign Authority → Set Guardrails → Resource → Checkpoint → Support → Review → Adjust**

This is a book working model, not a validated delegation taxonomy.

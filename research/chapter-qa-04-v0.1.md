# Chapter 4 QA — v0.1

## QA result

**Overall: PASS WITH REVISIONS**

Chapter 4 successfully turns resilience into an adaptive self-regulation problem rather than a “never give up” doctrine.

## 1. Conceptual QA — PASS

Core distinction is strong:

> **Persist on the goal; adapt the method.**

The chapter also correctly separates:
- discipline;
- consistency;
- persistence;
- rigidity;
- lapse;
- abandonment;
- goal failure;
- method failure;
- context failure.

This is one of the most important conceptual chapters in Part I because it prevents later chapters from turning persistence into solution attachment.

## 2. Evidence QA — PASS WITH REVISIONS

The evidence map supports:
- self-control as goal-aligned behavior;
- dynamic self-regulation;
- persistence plus timely disengagement;
- goal disengagement/reengagement;
- lapse and maintenance difficulties.

The chapter correctly avoids claiming that discipline alone causes success.

### Required evidence boundary

The four-category failure model:

1. execution failure;
2. method failure;
3. goal failure;
4. context failure;

is a **book diagnostic framework**, not a validated four-category taxonomy.

Add explicit wording:

> “Empat kategori ini adalah alat diagnosis praktis buku, bukan klasifikasi psikologis yang telah divalidasi sebagai satu model.”

## 3. Mechanism QA — PASS

The mechanism is coherent:

**goal → commitment → competing demands/friction → execution → monitoring → lapse/feedback → review → continue/adapt/pause/disengage → reengage**

This is compatible with dynamic self-regulation evidence while remaining clearly a book synthesis.

## 4. User failure-pattern QA — PASS

The pattern:

**ngotot di awal → lemas di tengah → menghindar → putus → post-hoc rationalization**

is used appropriately as a training case.

It is not presented as a clinical diagnosis or empirical prevalence claim.

This should remain explicit.

## 5. Important boundary: persistence vs sunk cost

The chapter handles sunk-cost risk well, but the “would I choose this today?” question must remain a **decision aid**, not a standalone decision rule.

Continuation can still be rational when future benefits justify future costs even if past costs are unrecoverable.

## 6. Minimum viable continuation

Useful and well connected to lapse recovery.

However, this is a pedagogical design rather than a validated universal intervention.

Keep the language:
- “salah satu cara”;
- “dapat membantu”;
- “desain praktik”.

Avoid:
- “cara yang terbukti”;
- “pasti membuat konsisten”.

## 7. Goal discipline

Strong canonical alignment with `DECISION.GOAL_DISCIPLINE`.

Important:
- do not let “goal discipline” become “never change the goal”;
- goal validity must remain reviewable.

## 9. Red-team

### “Is this just motivation?”
No. It emphasizes diagnosis, feedback, constraints, and method adaptation.

### “Does this teach people never to quit?”
No.

### “Does this excuse every abandonment?”
No. Execution failure remains a possible explanation.

### “Does this equate consistency with same behavior?”
No.

### “Does this encourage endless analysis?”
Potential risk. Checkpoints should lead to a decision: continue, adapt, pause, disengage, or reengage.

### “Does minimum viable continuation become an excuse for doing almost nothing?”
Possible risk. The concept should retain “meaningful” action and periodic review.

## 10. Practice QA

Commitment Check is strongly aligned with the chapter.

Required addition:
- “Evidence currently supporting the goal/method.”

This prevents the exercise from becoming motivation-only.

Recommended fields:

- Goal
- Why
- Evidence
- Minimum
- Friction
- Checkpoint
- Method update trigger
- Exit condition

## 11. Terminology QA

Monitor:
- resilience vs persistence;
- persistence vs rigidity;
- consistency vs repetition;
- discipline vs motivation;
- goal discipline vs stubbornness;
- recovery vs restart;
- pause vs abandonment;
- adaptation vs lack of commitment.

No architecture change required.

## 12. Editorial QA — PASS WITH REVISIONS

Strong:
- concrete;
- highly relevant to the book's intended training role;
- avoids inspirational cliché;
- contains practical decision questions.

Potential refinement:

- preserve dense examples;
- avoid overusing English labels when Indonesian is sufficient.

# Evidence Update — Chapter 38: Strategi Berarti Memilih

## Evidence focus

Chapter 38 treats strategy as a pattern of consequential choices about where to focus, what to pursue, what not to pursue, how to create or protect advantage, and how to align resources with those choices.

### Core evidence posture

- Strategy involves choices and trade-offs, not merely aspirations or a list of initiatives.
- A strategic choice becomes meaningful when it changes resource allocation, capabilities, activities, positioning, or priorities.
- Strategy operates under uncertainty and constraints; it cannot guarantee outcomes.
- Coherence across choices matters: one initiative can undermine another when they require incompatible capabilities, resources, or positions.
- Strategic choices should be connected to a diagnosis of the situation rather than copied from generic best practices.
- “Doing everything” can dilute resources and reduce the ability to execute a coherent position.
- Not choosing can itself function as a choice when resources and attention are finite.
- Strategic choices should be revisited when material assumptions or the environment changes, but constant switching can destroy coherence.
- Strategy and execution are interdependent: a strategy that cannot be supported by capabilities/resources is not operationally credible.

## Editorial implications

1. Start with a diagnosis, not a slogan.
2. Define the objective and the scope of choice.
3. Identify constraints and non-negotiables.
4. Generate materially different strategic options.
5. State what each option requires and what it gives up.
6. Test coherence across choices.
7. Allocate resources consistently with the chosen direction.
8. Identify leading assumptions and review triggers.
9. Distinguish strategic choice from annual planning or task lists.
10. Avoid “best strategy” language without specifying objective, context, constraints, and time horizon.

## Proposed book synthesis

**Diagnosis → Objective → Choice Set → Trade-offs → Coherence → Resource Commitment → Execution Logic → Assumptions → Review/Adapt**

This is a book working model, not a validated strategy taxonomy.

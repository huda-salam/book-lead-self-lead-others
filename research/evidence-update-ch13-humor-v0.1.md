# Evidence Update — Chapter 13: Humor, Cletukan, dan Batas yang Aman

## Evidence posture

Humor in leadership is context-dependent. The literature supports potential benefits but does not justify a universal “humor makes leaders better” claim.

## Key evidence

1. **Organizational humor review**
   Rosenberg, Walker, Leiter & Graffam's scoping review identifies humor styles/outcomes, humor as communication, boundary conditions, and cultural context as major themes. It emphasizes the multifaceted nature of humor. citeturn3search11

2. **Positive workplace humor meta-analysis**
   A meta-analysis of 49 studies and 8,532 participants found positive associations between workplace/supervisor humor and several work outcomes, while explicitly calling for attention to negative humor, moderators, and legal/ethical risks. citeturn3search3

3. **Organizational humor foundation review**
   The Annual Review treatment describes humor as potentially useful for communication, trust, and cooperation while emphasizing its complexity and risks. citeturn3search4

4. **Leader humor and psychological safety**
   A 2025 European Management Journal study using three-wave survey data from 583 employees reported different relationships for affiliative and aggressive leader humor, with affiliative humor linked to psychological safety and innovative behavior in its tested model. citeturn3search1

5. **Leader humor and incivility**
   A 2023 study with 290 employees reported a mediated relationship between leader humor and employee incivility through psychological safety in its tested model. This is a specific empirical model, not evidence that humor generally causes incivility. citeturn3search15

6. **Leader self-deprecating humor**
   A 2026 study with 197 respondents found relationships between leader self-deprecating humor and employee well-being/psychological safety mediated by leader-member exchange. The design is survey-based and should not be generalized as a universal causal rule. citeturn3search9

7. **Recent evidence on double-edged effects**
   A 2025/2026 study reports beneficial indirect effects for affiliative leader humor but inconsistent detrimental effects for aggressive humor, reinforcing the need for contextual interpretation. citeturn3search12

## Editorial implications

- Do not equate humor with positive leadership.
- Distinguish affiliative, self-directed/self-deprecating, aggressive, and situational humor where useful.
- Treat “target + power + context + cost” as the book's practical synthesis, not a validated taxonomy.
- Avoid claiming that laughter proves acceptance.
- Preserve cultural/contextual uncertainty.
- Humor should not replace feedback, accountability, safety communication, or conflict intervention.

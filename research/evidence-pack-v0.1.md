## 1. Bab 5 — Positive, Tetapi Tidak Menipu Diri

### Claim candidates
1. Optimism is not equivalent to ignoring negative information.
2. Optimism has been associated with approach-oriented coping and lower avoidance, but effects are correlational/construct-dependent.
3. Unrealistic optimism can have costs; therefore the book should distinguish constructive optimism from reality-denying optimism.
4. “Realism” itself should not be presented as a single universally measurable mental state.

### Evidence
- Nes & Segerstrom (2006), meta-analysis, K=50, N=11,629: dispositional optimism was positively associated with approach coping (r=.17) and negatively with avoidance coping (r=-.21). The review also reports that optimists may adjust coping strategies to situational demands.
- Bortolotti & Antrobus (2015) review: cautions against broad conclusions about realism/optimism and notes that unrealistic optimism remains robust across tasks while its benefits and costs are context-dependent.
- Schneider (2001) distinguishes realistic optimism from optimism involving self-deception and argues that realism and optimism need not be opposites.
- Dhami et al. / related work on mistaken expectations should be used cautiously; the British longitudinal study found both optimistic and pessimistic expectation errors associated with lower long-run well-being, but this is a specific population and operationalization.

### Boundary
Do not teach “always think positive.” The chapter should teach:
**lihat realitas → akui ketidakpastian → pertahankan kemungkinan perbaikan → pilih tindakan yang masih masuk akal.**

### Practice hypothesis
Use four boxes:
**Baik / Buruk / Belum diketahui / Masih dapat diubah.**

## 3. Bab 19 — Batas: Tegas Tanpa Menjadi Kasar

### Claim candidates
1. Boundary-setting is not equivalent to aggression.
2. Effective boundary behavior must communicate a limit and, where relevant, a consequence or escalation path.
3. The appropriate response depends on authority, stakes, safety, and relationship.

### Research requirement
Add evidence on assertiveness, interpersonal effectiveness, refusal behavior, and workplace boundary-setting before strong claims.

### Practice
**State limit → give relevant reason if useful → offer permissible alternative → state next step if boundary is crossed.**

Boundary language must not imply that every difficult situation can be solved by better wording.

## 4. Bab 20 — Memperbaiki Hubungan Setelah Kesalahan

### Evidence
- Experimental evidence shows apologies can promote forgiveness, with relationship value proposed as one causal pathway.
- A 2025 meta-analysis of 22 studies found that emotional apologies generally facilitated greater forgiveness, while also noting limitations in the range of emotions and perspectives studied.
- Trust-repair research indicates that the appropriate response can depend on the type of violation and subsequent evidence; apology is not a universal substitute for factual accountability.
- Research on competence- versus integrity-based violations indicates that apology/denial effects differ by violation type and evidence of guilt/innocence.

### Core implication
Repair should not be reduced to:
**“minta maaf → selesai.”**

Use:
**Acknowledge → clarify responsibility → apologize where warranted → correct → make restitution where appropriate → follow up → accept that trust recovery may take time.**

### Boundary
Forgiveness, trust repair, reconciliation, and restored performance are distinct outcomes.

## 5. Bab 23 — Mencari Akar Tanpa Terjebak Root Cause

### Research requirement
Need stronger evidence packet on:
- causal inference versus causal storytelling;
- multiple sufficient/contributing causes;
- counterfactual reasoning;
- common-cause/confounding errors;
- causal diagrams as practical reasoning tools.

### Provisional canonical rule
“Root cause” should be treated as a **useful operational question**, not as proof that every complex event has one unique deepest cause.

### Practice
**Observed outcome → candidate causes → evidence for/against → dependencies/confounders → intervention point → uncertainty.**

## 6. Bab 27 — Organisasi Bukan Hanya Struktur Resmi

### Research requirement
Need evidence on:
- informal networks;
- advice/friendship/communication networks;
- information flow;
- norms;
- incentives;
- formal versus informal structure.

### Provisional claim
Formal charts describe authority and reporting relationships; they do not necessarily describe all information, trust, influence, or coordination pathways.

### Practice
For a real issue, map:
**formal owner / actual information source / affected party / blocker / influencer / decision authority.**

Do not equate “informal” with “bad” or “political.”

## 9. Bab 36 — Delegasi Bukan Memindahkan Beban

### Evidence
Delegation literature distinguishes transfer of authority from merely transferring responsibility. The practical problem is not simply “give someone work,” but matching authority, ownership, capability, and control mechanisms.

### Core model
**Outcome → authority → capability fit → constraints → checkpoint → feedback → accountability.**

### Boundary
Delegation is not abdication; micromanagement is not the only alternative. The appropriate checkpoint density depends on task risk, capability, novelty, and reversibility.

## 10. Bab 37 — Melihat Dua Langkah ke Depan

### Research requirement
Need stronger evidence on:
- second-order effects;
- unintended consequences;
- scenario thinking;
- systems thinking;
- delayed feedback.

### Provisional practice
For each major option ask:
1. What happens immediately?
2. What does that cause next?
3. Who adapts to our action?
4. What incentive changes?
5. What new constraint appears?
6. What becomes harder to reverse?

This is a reasoning tool, not a prediction engine.

## 11. Bab 41–42 — Belajar dari Manusia

### Canonical evidence rule
A biography, religious narrative, historical account, interview, or success story is not automatically causal evidence.

Each exemplar should be processed through:
**Source → Context → Observable action → Claimed principle → Alternative explanations → Supporting evidence → Boundary → Transfer question.**

### Bab 41
For Islamic exemplars, separate:
- Qur'an;
- authenticated hadith;
- classical interpretation;
- historical/sirah reports;
- later retellings;
- modern interpretation.

Do not silently collapse these into one evidence tier.

### Bab 42
For modern exemplars, explicitly guard against:
- survivorship bias;
- halo effect;
- retrospective storytelling;
- selection bias;
- success-story causal inflation.

The question is not “How did this person become great?” but:
**“Apa yang benar-benar dapat kita pelajari dari perilaku ini, dalam konteks apa, dengan evidence sekuat apa, dan apa batas transfernya?”**

## 12. Evidence-to-book translation rule

For every research item, final chapter drafting must use:

**Evidence shows → Book interpretation → Boundary → Practical implication**

Not:

**Interesting study → motivational slogan.**

The book's conceptual model remains a pedagogical synthesis. Evidence should calibrate its claims, not be forced to prove the entire model.

# Evidence Update — Chapter 26: Ketika Keputusan Tidak Menghasilkan Outcome yang Diinginkan

## Evidence focus

Chapter 26 addresses post-decision review: what a leader does when an outcome differs from what was intended.

### 1. Outcome quality and decision quality are different questions

A poor outcome does not automatically prove that the original decision was irrational. A good outcome does not automatically prove that the decision process was sound. Review should reconstruct what was knowable, assumed, decided, and executed at the time.

### 2. Review should distinguish decision, execution, and context

An undesired result can arise from:
- decision quality;
- execution quality;
- changed external conditions;
- incorrect assumptions;
- measurement problems;
- interaction among these factors.

This prevents hindsight from collapsing all failure into “bad decision.”

### 3. Feedback supports adaptive self-regulation

Self-regulation research treats goals, feedback, discrepancies, competing goals, and changing conditions as dynamic. Review therefore should produce updated information and future action rather than merely assign blame.

### 4. Reflection can help or harm

Reflection can support learning and problem solving, but repetitive negative rumination can be counterproductive. The book should therefore distinguish structured review from unbounded self-criticism.

### 5. Persistence requires review and possible disengagement

Goal pursuit research indicates that persistence and timely disengagement/reengagement can complement one another. Continuing indefinitely is not automatically evidence of commitment.

### 6. Review should end in an update

A useful review changes at least one of:
- decision rule;
- assumption;
- method;
- resource allocation;
- monitoring;
- goal;
- or decision to stop/redirect.

## Editorial implications

- Reconstruct the decision using information available at the time.
- Separate outcome, decision, execution, and context.
- Ask what was predicted and what actually happened.
- Examine disconfirming evidence that was available before the decision.
- Identify unexpected events without using hindsight as proof.
- Distinguish learning from blame.
- Convert lessons into changed practice.
- Define stop/change/continue criteria.
- Avoid “failure = person failure” framing.

## Proposed book synthesis

**Outcome → Expectation → Decision → Execution → Context → Evidence → Attribution → Update → Continue/Change/Stop**

This is a book working model, not a validated review taxonomy.

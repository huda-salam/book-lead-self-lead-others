# Evidence Update — Chapter 33: Membuat Orang Lain Bertumbuh

## Evidence focus

Chapter 33 treats developing others as a leadership capability: increasing another person's capability, judgment, confidence, and ownership over time without creating dependency or confusing support with rescuing.

### 1. Development is more than training

People develop through a combination of challenging work, feedback, practice, reflection, coaching, observation, and opportunities to make decisions. Formal training is only one mechanism.

### 2. Development requires an appropriate level of challenge

Too little challenge produces limited growth. Too much challenge without support can create avoidable failure or overload. The appropriate level depends on capability, task, stakes, and context.

### 3. Feedback and coaching serve different functions

Feedback provides information about behavior, impact, standards, or performance. Coaching can help a person think through options, assumptions, and solutions. Leaders should not turn every coaching conversation into a lecture.

### 4. Autonomy should increase with capability and evidence

Delegation and autonomy can develop judgment when the person has appropriate authority, information, and support. Development does not mean abandoning people to “learn the hard way.”

### 5. Psychological safety supports learning but does not remove accountability

People need enough interpersonal safety to ask questions, admit uncertainty, and surface mistakes. Standards and accountability remain necessary.

### 6. Development includes failure with bounded consequences

Learning opportunities should be designed so that mistakes do not create disproportionate harm. High-risk work may require closer supervision or staged delegation.

### 7. Individual differences matter

People differ in prior experience, motivation, learning needs, confidence, and context. A single development method should not be treated as universally effective.

### 8. Leader development includes succession and redundancy

A leader who becomes the only person who can solve a problem creates organizational dependency. Developing others increases resilience and reduces key-person risk.

## Editorial implications

- Develop judgment, not just task compliance.
- Match challenge and support to context.
- Use questions before giving answers where appropriate.
- Give actionable feedback.
- Increase autonomy gradually where evidence supports it.
- Define guardrails and escalation paths.
- Avoid creating dependency on the leader.
- Give credit for growth.
- Treat mistakes as learning only within proportionate risk boundaries.

## Proposed book synthesis

**Diagnose → Set Challenge → Clarify Guardrails → Practice → Feedback → Reflect → Increase Autonomy → Review**

This is a book working model, not a validated development taxonomy.

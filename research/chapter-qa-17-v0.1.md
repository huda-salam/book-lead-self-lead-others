Status: **PASS WITH REVISIONS**

## Findings

1. **Leader capability framing — PASS**  
   Conflict is treated as something a leader must read and manage proportionately, not eliminate or celebrate.

2. **Boundary with Chapter 11 — PASS**  
   Respectful disagreement remains Chapter 11; Chapter 17 expands the analysis to conflict type, interests, resources, authority, and risk.

3. **Boundary with Chapter 15 — PASS**  
   Difficult conversations remain Chapter 15; Chapter 17 focuses on understanding the conflict system before choosing a conversation or other intervention.

4. **Boundary with Chapter 16 — PASS**  
   Relational conditions remain Chapter 16; Chapter 17 examines conflict itself.

5. **Boundary with Chapter 18 — PASS**  
   Chapter 17 reads and classifies the conflict. Chapter 18 will own de-escalation mechanics.

6. **Boundary with Chapter 19 — PASS**  
   Boundaries are introduced only as one possible response; Chapter 19 owns boundary-setting in depth.

7. **Mechanism — PASS**  
   The chapter distinguishes facts, interpretations, goals, processes, resources, interests, authority, and risk rather than reducing conflict to personality.

8. **Evidence posture — PASS WITH PROVENANCE CONDITION**  
   Evidence is used cautiously: conflict types have different relationships with outcomes and are context-dependent. No claim is made that task conflict is inherently beneficial or relationship conflict is the only harmful form.

9. **Power and symmetry — PASS**  
   The chapter explicitly notes that authority and information may be distributed differently, without assuming motives.

10. **Practical framework — PASS**  
    `What differs? → Why? → Stakes → Evidence → Negotiable? → Decision/Boundary? → Proportionate response` is clearly labeled as a book synthesis, not a validated taxonomy.

11. **Anti-romanticization / anti-avoidance — PASS**  
    The chapter avoids both “conflict is always bad” and “conflict is always productive,” while preserving escalation/formal action for serious cases.

12. **Red-team — PASS WITH MINOR PROVENANCE CONDITION**  
    Final publication requires bibliographic verification and careful handling of older meta-analysis findings versus newer evidence.

# Chapter 31 QA — Ownership dan Kredibilitas

Status: **PASS WITH REVISIONS**

## Findings

1. **Leader capability framing — PASS**  
   Ownership and credibility are treated as behavioral capabilities rather than personality labels.

2. **Ownership versus blame — PASS**  
   The chapter explicitly distinguishes accurate ownership from total self-blame and blame shifting.

3. **Control/influence boundary — PASS**  
   Responsibility is bounded by authority, information, influence, and reasonable control while preserving responsibility for decisions and escalation within one's role.

4. **Credibility versus confidence — PASS**  
   Calibrated uncertainty is treated as compatible with credibility; confidence is not treated as proof.

5. **Follow-through — PASS**  
   Credibility is linked to observable commitments and delivery rather than declarations.

6. **Bad-news handling — PASS**  
   Surfacing material risks early is framed as part of ownership, without requiring a perfect solution before escalation.

7. **Credit/accountability — PASS**  
   The chapter guards against both credit-taking and blame-shifting.

8. **Consistency versus rigidity — PASS**  
   Principles can remain stable while methods adapt when evidence or context changes.

9. **Repair — PASS**  
   Credibility repair is linked to changed behavior over time rather than apology alone, connecting appropriately to Chapter 20.

10. **Practical framework — PASS**  
    `Commitment → Clarity → Follow-through → Transparency → Accountability → Repair → Consistency` is explicitly a book synthesis, not a validated credibility taxonomy.

11. **Boundary with Chapters 25–26 and 32–34 — PASS**  
    Execution, review, influence, development, and pressure remain distinct ownership areas.

12. **Evidence posture — PASS WITH PROVENANCE CONDITION**  
    Final publication requires bibliography verification for credibility, trust, reliability, accountability, transparency, and leader behavior claims.

# Chapter 7 QA — v0.1

## QA result

**Overall: PASS WITH REVISIONS**

Chapter 7 establishes READ.OBSERVATION and LITERACY.DISCIPLINE clearly and provides a strong bridge from emotional regulation to evidence-aware interpretation.

## 1. Conceptual QA — PASS

The core separation is useful:

**Observation → Interpretation → Hypothesis → Unknown**

The chapter correctly avoids the false implication that interpretation can or should be eliminated.

The central discipline is:

> “Jangan lupa bahwa interpretasi adalah interpretasi.”

## 2. Evidence QA — PASS WITH REVISIONS

The cited self-regulation and reflection literature supports the broader claim that interpretation, cognition, emotion, and behavior interact dynamically.

However, the exact four-layer framework is a **book synthesis**, not a validated psychological taxonomy.

Likewise, the statement:

> “Semakin jauh kesimpulan dari observasi, semakin penting evidence tambahan.”

is a sound epistemic rule but should remain clearly a book principle rather than a directly empirical finding.

## 3. Literacy discipline — PASS

The chapter successfully operationalizes:

> **Kesimpulan tidak boleh lebih kuat daripada kualitas, cakupan, dan kebaruan knowledge yang mendukungnya.**

The three checks:
- quality;
- scope;
- currency;

are useful and should remain.

One refinement: evidence quality also depends on fit to the claim. Add or preserve **relevance/directness** as a consideration.

## 4. Uncertainty boundary — PASS

Strong distinction:

> uncertainty ≠ equal plausibility.

This prevents epistemic humility from becoming indiscriminate skepticism.

The chapter correctly allows differential plausibility while requiring evidence.

## 5. Alternative explanations — PASS

The “one or two alternatives” heuristic is useful.

Boundary:
- it is a reasoning aid;
- not every situation requires multiple explicit hypotheses;
- alternatives should be plausible enough to test, not invented merely to create doubt.

## 6. Labels and organizational context — PASS WITH REVISIONS

The treatment of “malas”, “toxic”, “manipulatif”, “tidak kompeten”, etc. is aligned with the book's defensive literacy.

Important boundary:
- observable repeated behavior can legitimately support managerial decisions;
- the chapter should not imply that avoiding labels means avoiding accountability.

Recommended principle:

> “Turunkan label menjadi perilaku yang dapat diamati sebelum menentukan tindakan.”

## 7. Analysis avoidance — PASS

Strong addition.

Literacy discipline must not become endless research.

The chapter correctly ends analysis with:

> “Apa keputusan yang perlu dibuat dengan informasi yang tersedia sekarang?”

This connects to Ch9 and later decision chapters.

## 9. Practice QA

Observation Log is strongly linked to the mechanism.

The “Turunkan satu tingkat kepastian” exercise is especially useful because it changes language and epistemic posture simultaneously.

Keep both explicitly as book exercises, not validated instruments.

## 10. Red-team

### Does this teach “facts only” thinking?
No.

### Does it make all interpretations equal?
No.

### Does it make people unable to act?
No; analysis avoidance is explicitly addressed.

### Does it encourage distrust of labels even when repeated behavior matters?
Potential risk. Add behavior-based accountability boundary.

### Does it encourage over-analysis?
Potential risk, mitigated by the decision question.

### Does “unknown” become a permanent excuse?
No, provided next-information and action fields remain.

## 11. Retrieval QA

### Questions
- “Apakah saya benar-benar tahu atau hanya menyimpulkan?”
- “Bagaimana membedakan fakta dan asumsi?”
- “Saya merasa dia sengaja—bagaimana mengeceknya?”
- “Apakah tidak adanya bukti berarti bukti tidak ada?”
- “Bagaimana mencari alternative explanation?”

### Cases
- message not answered;
- superior asks for meeting;
- colleague misses meetings;
- sales fall;
- someone called “toxic/manipulative”.

### Red flags
- “Dia pasti…”
- “Mereka sengaja…”
- “Saya tahu orang seperti itu.”
- “Tidak ada bukti, tapi saya bisa merasakan.”
- “Saya sudah baca satu artikel, berarti sudah terbukti.”

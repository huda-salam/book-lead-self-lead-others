# Evidence Update — Chapter 15: Feedback dan Percakapan Sulit

## Evidence focus

Bab ini membahas feedback sebagai pertukaran informasi untuk memperbaiki pemahaman, perilaku, keputusan, atau hasil kerja; serta percakapan sulit sebagai situasi ketika stakes, emosi, perbedaan kepentingan, atau konsekuensi membuat komunikasi membutuhkan judgment lebih tinggi.

### 1. Feedback is not uniformly beneficial
A large systematic review and meta-analysis of supervisory feedback published in 2026 synthesized 24 studies, 75 effect sizes, and 595,950 observations. The review examined multiple feedback characteristics rather than treating feedback as a single intervention, supporting a differentiated treatment of source, content, delivery, timing, and context. citeturn0search0turn0search1

### 2. Feedback interventions can have unintended effects
Kluger & DeNisi's classic meta-analysis of feedback interventions found substantial variability and documented that feedback interventions can sometimes reduce performance, challenging the simplistic assumption that more feedback is always better. This supports the chapter's emphasis on purpose, task relevance, recipient interpretation, and consequences. citeturn0search2

### 3. Feedback seeking is distinct from feedback giving
An integrative review by Anseel et al. (2015) distinguishes feedback seeking from feedback giving and emphasizes motives, costs, source, timing, and consequences. This supports treating the recipient as an active participant rather than a passive container for correction. citeturn0search3

### 4. Feedback conversations are relational
Research on feedback environment and feedback orientation suggests that trust, source credibility, availability, and relational context can shape how feedback is received and used. The chapter should therefore avoid reducing difficult conversations to wording alone. citeturn0search4turn0search5

### 5. Psychological safety does not remove accountability
Psychological safety research supports the importance of interpersonal safety for speaking up and learning, but it should not be equated with absence of performance standards, disagreement, or consequences. citeturn0search6

### 6. Difficult conversations involve competing goals
Conflict and negotiation literature suggests that difficult conversations may involve simultaneous concerns for task outcomes, relationship quality, identity, fairness, and face. A useful practical implication is to separate the substantive issue from unnecessary identity threat without pretending that all conflict can or should be made comfortable. citeturn0search7

## Editorial implications

- Feedback should have a reason; avoid ritualized feedback for its own sake.
- Separate observation from interpretation and judgment, consistent with Chapter 7.
- Describe behavior/result and impact before assigning character labels.
- Distinguish correction, appreciation, coaching, warning, evaluation, and decision communication.
- Feedback can be positive, negative, mixed, or corrective; do not equate feedback with criticism.
- The receiver's response is data, not automatic proof that the feedback was right or wrong.
- Preserve room for disagreement and evidence.
- Difficult conversations may require boundaries, escalation, documentation, or decisions rather than further discussion.
- Avoid universal claims that a particular feedback formula works for everyone.
- Do not frame “radical candor,” “sandwich feedback,” or any other branded formula as universally optimal.

## Proposed book synthesis

A practical conversation loop:

**Purpose → Observation → Impact → Perspective → Request → Response → Agreement/Decision → Follow-up**

This is a book working model, not a validated taxonomy.

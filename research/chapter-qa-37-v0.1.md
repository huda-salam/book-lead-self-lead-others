# Chapter 37 QA — Melihat Dua Langkah ke Depan

Status: **PASS WITH REVISIONS**

## Findings

1. **Strategic capability framing — PASS**  
   Second-order thinking is framed as reasoning about plausible downstream consequences, not forecasting certainty.

2. **Behavioral adaptation — PASS**  
   The chapter explicitly considers how people and systems adapt to rules, incentives, targets, and controls.

3. **Direct versus downstream effects — PASS**  
   First-order and second-order effects are distinguished without implying that every downstream effect is predictable.

4. **Feedback loops and dependencies — PASS**  
   The chapter identifies reinforcing/balancing effects, bottleneck migration, dependencies, and changes to the decision environment.

5. **Positive and negative downstream effects — PASS**  
   Second-order effects are not treated as synonymous with unintended harm; opportunities are also considered.

6. **Anti-overthinking boundary — PASS**  
   The chapter limits analysis to plausible, relevant, material consequences and suggests monitoring when uncertainty remains.

7. **Reversibility — PASS**  
   Reversible pilots are presented as a way to reduce the cost of being wrong when stakes permit.

8. **Scenario/pre-mortem boundary — PASS**  
   These are presented as tools for surfacing blind spots, not prediction machines.

9. **Indicators and review triggers — PASS**  
   The chapter converts uncertainty into observable indicators and explicit review triggers.

10. **Framework — PASS**  
    `Decision → First-order Effect → Adaptation → Second-order Effect → Interaction → Risk/Opportunity → Indicator → Review` is explicitly a book synthesis, not a validated taxonomy.

11. **Boundary with Chapters 24, 25, 26, 35, 38 — PASS**  
    Trade-offs, execution, review, prioritization, and strategic choice retain distinct ownership.

12. **Evidence posture — PASS WITH PROVENANCE CONDITION**  
    Final publication requires bibliography verification for systems thinking, incentives, behavioral adaptation, scenario analysis, premortem, reversibility, and adaptive decision-making claims.

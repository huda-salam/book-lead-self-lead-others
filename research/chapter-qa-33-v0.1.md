## Findings

1. **Leader capability framing — PASS**  
   Development is framed as increasing another person's capability, judgment, and ownership rather than merely extracting more output.

2. **Training versus development — PASS**  
   Formal training is distinguished from practice, challenge, feedback, reflection, and real work.

3. **Challenge/support balance — PASS**  
   The chapter avoids claiming one universal optimal level of challenge and explicitly conditions it on capability, task, stakes, and context.

4. **Coaching/directing boundary — PASS**  
   Questions are encouraged where they build judgment, but direct instruction remains legitimate when capability, urgency, or stakes require it.

5. **Safety/accountability — PASS**  
   Psychological safety supports learning but does not remove standards or accountability.

6. **Delegation boundary — PASS**  
   Development is distinguished from delegation; Chapter 36 retains ownership of delegation as a management mechanism.

7. **Dependency risk — PASS**  
   The chapter explicitly identifies leader-created dependency and key-person bottlenecks as development failures.

8. **Risk-bounded learning — PASS**  
   Developmental mistakes are conditioned by cost of failure; high-risk contexts may require tighter supervision.

9. **Judgment over mere task compliance — PASS**  
   Development includes knowing when to apply a procedure, when to question, and when to escalate.

10. **Practical framework — PASS**  
    `Diagnose → Set Challenge → Clarify Guardrails → Practice → Feedback → Reflect → Increase Autonomy → Review` is explicitly a book synthesis, not a validated development taxonomy.

11. **Boundary with Chapters 15, 31, 32, 34 — PASS**  
    Feedback, credibility, influence, and pressure remain distinct leadership capabilities.

12. **Evidence posture — PASS WITH PROVENANCE CONDITION**  
    Final publication requires bibliography verification for coaching, developmental challenge, feedback, autonomy, workplace learning, and leadership development claims.

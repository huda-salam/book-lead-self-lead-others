# Evidence Update v0.6 — Goal Pursuit, Adjustment, and Lapse Recovery

## Evidence records

### E-DISC-01 — Self-control and valued goals
- Source: Duckworth et al. (2019), Annual Review of Psychology.
- Finding: self-control concerns aligning thoughts, feelings, and actions with enduringly valued goals despite more immediately attractive alternatives; the review reports longitudinal associations with academic attainment.
- Supports: the conceptualization of self-discipline as goal-aligned execution under competing short-term attractions.
- Does not establish: that discipline alone determines achievement or that an identical mechanism transfers unchanged to every workplace context. citeturn0search0

### E-DISC-02 — Implementation intentions
- Source: Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006), Advances in Experimental Social Psychology meta-analysis.
- Scope: 94 independent tests.
- Finding: implementation intentions showed a positive effect on goal attainment in the reviewed literature and were examined as a mechanism for initiating goal striving, shielding pursuit from unwanted influences, and disengaging from failing courses of action.
- Supports: using if-then planning as one practice mechanism in Chapter 44.
- Does not establish: that if-then planning guarantees long-term behavior change or that its effect is identical across all populations/goals. citeturn1search0turn1search1

### E-DISC-03 — Persistence and disengagement
- Source: Brandstätter & Bernecker (2022), Annual Review of Psychology.
- Finding: persistence and timely disengagement are treated as complementary components of self-regulation; the review covers situational and personal determinants and identifies unresolved questions.
- Supports: teaching persistence together with the ability to disengage or revise.
- Does not establish: a universal rule for when an individual should stop pursuing a goal. citeturn0search1turn0search35

### E-DISC-04 — Goal adjustment meta-analysis
- Source: 2025 meta-analytic review indexed in PubMed, 235 studies and 1,421 effect sizes.
- Finding: goal disengagement, reengagement, and goal-striving flexibility have distinct antecedents and outcomes; overall evidence quality was judged low to moderate because of cross-sectional designs, publication-bias risk, and heterogeneity.
- Supports: goal adjustment as a legitimate component of self-regulation.
- Does not establish: that every goal change is adaptive or that one adjustment strategy is universally superior. citeturn1search2

### E-DISC-05 — Goal adjustment and quality of life
- Source: Barlow, Wrosch & McGrath (2020), meta-analytic review.
- Scope: 31 samples, 421 effect sizes.
- Finding: goal disengagement and reengagement capacities were associated with quality-of-life indicators, with moderators and differences across outcomes.
- Supports: distinguishing persistence from the ability to disengage/reengage.
- Does not establish: direct workplace-performance causality. citeturn1search4

### E-DISC-06 — Habit formation
- Source: 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis of health-behavior habit formation, 20 studies and 2,601 participants.
- Finding: habit scores improved across included interventions, but most studies had high risk of bias; reported time to habit formation varied substantially between people and behaviors.
- Supports: designing maintenance around repeated behavior and context rather than assuming motivation will remain high.
- Does not establish: a universal “habit takes X days” rule. citeturn1search8

### E-DISC-07 — Lapse and relapse
- Source: Roordink et al. (2023), systematic review of prospective adult studies on physical activity and dietary behavior.
- Finding: maintenance is difficult and relapse has multiple prospective predictors.
- Supports: Chapter 44 should include lapse recognition and recovery rather than treating one missed action as total failure.
- Does not establish: that a single recovery protocol works across domains. citeturn1search12

### E-DISC-08 — Dynamic self-regulation
- Source: Neal, Ballard & Vancouver (2017), Annual Review of Organizational Psychology and Organizational Behavior.
- Finding: self-regulation is dynamic and includes managing competing demands, selecting and sequencing goals/tasks, and adjusting goals.
- Supports: stable goal direction with adaptive execution.
- Does not establish: a single optimal personal operating system. citeturn0search2

## Revised design model

The book's practical model should now be:

**Choose a valid goal → define a realistic minimum action → anticipate friction → execute → monitor → recover from lapse → review evidence → continue, adapt, pause, or disengage → reengage where appropriate.**

This is a pedagogical synthesis informed by the evidence above, not a validated intervention.

## Key editorial rule

Avoid:
> “Kalau benar-benar disiplin, pasti berhasil.”

Prefer:
> “Disiplin membantu menjaga tindakan tetap selaras dengan tujuan, tetapi keberhasilan juga bergantung pada kualitas tujuan, metode, konteks, feedback, constraints, dan kemampuan menyesuaikan diri.”

## Chapter implications

- Ch 4: persistence must include adaptation and legitimate disengagement.
- Ch 22: solution attachment must be tested against goal validity.
- Ch 25: execution must include recovery and checkpoint design.
- Ch 26: review must distinguish effort failure, method failure, goal failure, and context failure.
- Ch 39: adaptation should be framed as disciplined updating, not lack of commitment.
- Ch 44: build a lapse-recovery loop, not a perfection loop.

## Remaining research gate

Still required before drafting:
- stronger workplace-specific evidence;
- adult goal pursuit outside academic/health domains;
- cultural/contextual moderators;
- evidence on maintenance after initial implementation;
- evidence on how goal, method, and resource constraints interact over long projects.

# Evidence Update — Chapter 41: Islamic Exemplars

## Status

Draft evidence packet v0.1.

## Editorial rule

Chapter 41 treats Qur'an, hadith, and early Islamic reports as sources within their own religious and historical authority. It does not convert a religious text into a modern psychological finding. Modern leadership and psychology concepts are analytical lenses only when explicitly labeled as interpretation.

## Primary sources

- Qur'an 33:21: prophetic example.
- Qur'an 3:159: gentleness, pardon, consultation, and resolve after decision.
- Qur'an 49:6: verification before consequential action.
- Sahih al-Bukhari 2698, 2700, 2711–2712, 2731–2732: Hudaibiyah negotiations and treaty terms.
- Sahih al-Bukhari 5729: Umar and the epidemic at Sargh.
- Sahih al-Bukhari 1399–1400 and 6924–6925: Abu Bakr and zakat after the Prophet's death.
- Sahih al-Bukhari 219, 220, 6025, 6128: Bedouin in the mosque.

## Source hierarchy

1. Qur'an for Qur'anic claims.
2. Sahih hadith for hadith claims, with collection and number.
3. Historical/sirah reports only when explicitly labeled.
4. Modern scholarship for interpretation or analytical framing.

## Transfer rules

- Do not invent quotations.
- Do not present paraphrase as verbatim quotation.
- Do not upgrade weak or historical reports into established hadith claims.
- Do not claim modern constructs were directly taught as scientific theories in scripture.
- Do not turn a prophetic action into a one-to-one management tactic without context.
- Do not treat Sahabah as interchangeable with the Prophet in authority.
- Do not turn historical narratives into personality diagnoses.

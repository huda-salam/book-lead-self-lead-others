# Evidence Update — Chapter 17: Konflik: Jangan Romantisasi, Jangan Takuti

## Evidence focus

Bab ini menempatkan konflik sebagai kondisi yang dapat muncul dari perbedaan tugas, proses, hubungan, status, kepentingan, sumber daya, atau interpretasi. Fokus leader bukan menghapus semua konflik, melainkan membedakan jenis, risiko, dan respons yang sesuai.

### 1. Conflict is multidimensional

Jehn and Mannix (2001) distinguish task, relationship, and process conflict and show that conflict types can have different temporal patterns and relationships with team performance. This supports avoiding the claim that “conflict is always bad” or “conflict is always productive.” citeturn0search0turn0search1

### 2. Meta-analytic evidence is more cautious about conflict benefits

A large body of meta-analytic research has found generally negative or weak average relationships between several forms of team conflict and performance, while moderators such as task interdependence, conflict type, and context matter. Recent meta-analysis also examines task, relationship, process, and status conflict as distinct forms rather than treating conflict as one construct. citeturn0search2turn0search3

### 3. Relationship conflict is especially costly

Meta-analytic work by De Dreu & Weingart (2003) found negative relationships between both relationship conflict and task conflict and team performance/satisfaction, with relationship conflict showing particularly consistent negative associations. The historical result should be presented with its age and methodological context rather than as a universal current law. citeturn0search4

### 4. Task conflict does not automatically improve decisions

Later research has challenged simple claims that task disagreement automatically improves performance. Benefits may depend on how disagreement is expressed, whether it remains focused on the task, and contextual conditions. This is consistent with the book's distinction between disagreement and relational attack.

### 5. Conflict can involve interests and structure, not merely communication

Conflict may arise from incompatible goals, scarce resources, authority boundaries, incentives, role ambiguity, dependency, or competing priorities. Therefore better wording alone cannot resolve every conflict.

## Editorial implications

- Do not romanticize conflict as inherently productive.
- Do not demonize conflict as inherently destructive.
- Distinguish task/content, process, relationship, status/power, and interest/resource dimensions as practical lenses, not a universal validated taxonomy for every conflict.
- Do not infer motive from conflict behavior.
- Separate disagreement from disrespect.
- Separate conflict existence from conflict escalation.
- A disagreement can be legitimate even when no party is acting badly.
- Some conflicts require negotiation, decision, boundary, structural redesign, or escalation rather than mutual emotional understanding.
- Preserve cultural and organizational context.
- Do not promise that conflict-resolution techniques can always produce win-win outcomes.

## Proposed book synthesis

A practical conflict-reading sequence:

**What differs? → Why does it differ? → What is at stake? → What is observable? → What can be negotiated? → What requires decision/boundary? → What response is proportionate?**

This is a book working model, not a validated taxonomy.

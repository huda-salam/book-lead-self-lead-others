# Chapter 29 QA — Ketika Kepentingan Bertabrakan

Status: **PASS WITH REVISIONS**

## Findings

1. **Leader capability framing — PASS**  
   Interest conflict is treated as a leadership decision and negotiation capability, not merely interpersonal conflict.

2. **Interest versus character — PASS**  
   Different interests are not automatically interpreted as bad motives.

3. **Position versus interest — PASS**  
   The chapter teaches movement from stated positions to interests and constraints while preserving the uncertainty of inferred motives.

4. **Negotiability boundary — PASS**  
   Legal, safety, mandatory policy, rights, and other legitimate hard constraints are not treated as ordinary bargaining variables.

5. **Win-win caution — PASS**  
   Integrative possibilities are tested but not assumed. Genuine zero-sum or incompatible choices remain possible.

6. **Power integration — PASS**  
   Alternatives and dependency connect Chapter 29 to the Power & Influence Map in Chapter 28 without duplicating it.

7. **Decision integration — PASS**  
   Trade-offs are made explicit, while general decision architecture remains owned by Chapter 24.

8. **Process legitimacy — PASS**  
   Criteria, voice, decision rights, explainability, and consistent application are treated as relevant process conditions without promising acceptance.

9. **Manipulation boundary — PASS**  
   Negotiation is explicitly separated from bluffing, coercion, deception, and other problematic influence tactics.

10. **Conflict of interest distinction — PASS**  
    Ordinary interest conflict is distinguished from conflict of interest that may require disclosure, recusal, or governance controls.

11. **Practical framework — PASS**  
    `Issue → Actors → Positions → Interests → Constraints → Alternatives → Trade-offs → Agreement/Decision → Follow-through` is explicitly a book synthesis, not a validated negotiation taxonomy.

12. **Evidence posture — PASS WITH PROVENANCE CONDITION**  
    Final publication requires bibliography verification for negotiation, integrative/distributive conflict, BATNA/alternatives, procedural justice, and organizational conflict.

# Chapter 8 QA — v0.1

## QA result

**Overall: PASS WITH REVISIONS**

Chapter 8 correctly owns READ.PERSPECTIVE and extends Chapter 7 from epistemic separation into interpersonal hypothesis-testing.

## 1. Conceptual QA — PASS

The central model is clear:

**Observe → Hypothesize → Ask → Test → Update**

It is explicitly identified as a book framework rather than a validated psychological procedure.

The chapter correctly distinguishes perspective-taking from mind-reading and from agreement.

## 2. Evidence QA — PASS WITH REVISIONS

Longmire & Harrison (2018) is appropriate for the distinction between perspective-taking and empathic concern and for context dependence.

Elfenbein (2023) supports the broader role of interpretation and context in workplace emotion.

Lord et al. (2010) is supporting background for dynamic self-regulation and interpretation, but should not be presented as direct validation of the chapter's five-step model.

Empirical findings must remain attached to their actual scope; the five-step model is book synthesis.

## 3. Perspective vs mind-reading — PASS

The canonical boundary is:

> “Perspektif adalah hipotesis yang dapat diperbaiki, bukan fakta tersembunyi yang kita miliki.”

## 4. Perspective vs agreement — PASS

The chapter explicitly states that understanding does not require agreement, trust, compliance, or surrender of constraints.

## 5. Context and power — PASS WITH REVISIONS

The section on positional differences is useful for organizational settings.

Refinement required: do not imply that every disagreement is merely a perspective difference. Some disputes concern objectively testable facts, authority, rules, resources, or incompatible interests.

Perspective-taking should supplement evidence and governance, not replace them.

## 6. Negative judgment / confirmation loop — PASS

The chapter's question about what evidence would change one's judgment is a practical anti-confirmation-bias heuristic.

Do not present it as a formal diagnostic test.

## 7. Character claims — PASS

The chapter correctly raises the evidence burden for broad character claims.

Repeated observable behavior may still justify accountability or managerial action without requiring a character diagnosis.

## 8. Empathy boundary — PASS

Calibrated language such as “mungkin”, “saya membayangkan”, and “apakah saya keliru?” appropriately avoids claiming direct access to another person's internal state.

## 9. Practice QA — PASS

Perspective Check directly mirrors the chapter mechanism and includes an update step.

Keep it as a learning exercise, not a validated instrument.

## 11. Red-team

- Does it teach mind-reading? No.
- Does it equate empathy with agreement? No.
- Does it make all perspectives equally valid? No.
- Could it encourage excessive analysis? Some risk, mitigated by treating the framework as a tool, not a ritual.
- Could it excuse harmful behavior? Limited risk; constraints and accountability remain explicit.

## 12. Retrieval QA

Questions:
- Bagaimana membaca orang tanpa sok tahu?
- Bagaimana mengetahui apakah saya sedang mind-reading?
- Bagaimana menguji dugaan tentang motif orang?
- Apakah memahami orang berarti harus setuju?
- Bagaimana menghadapi orang yang sudah saya nilai negatif?

Cases:
- colleague rejects a plan;
- superior requests more time;
- subordinate misses a deadline;
- stakeholder appears resistant;
- someone is labeled manipulative.

## 13. Terminology QA

Monitor perspective-taking vs empathy, hypothesis vs mind-reading, perspective vs agreement, behavior vs character, evidence vs intuition, and understanding vs accommodation.

## 14. Editorial QA — PASS WITH REVISIONS

The chapter is practical and avoids generic motivational prose.

One refinement remains: define “perspective-taking” once in Indonesian before using the English term repeatedly.

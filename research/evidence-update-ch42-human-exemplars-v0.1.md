# Evidence Update — Chapter 42: Human Exemplars

## Status

Evidence packet v0.1.

## Evidence requirements

Chapter 42 does not use a single famous-person example as proof of a general leadership claim. Each future exemplar must document:

1. source/provenance;
2. behavior;
3. context;
4. decision alternatives where recoverable;
5. mechanism hypothesis;
6. evidence;
7. alternative explanations;
8. outcome;
9. boundary;
10. transfer.

## Main risks

- survivorship bias;
- halo effect;
- selection bias;
- retrospective storytelling;
- outcome-to-cause inference;
- personality inference from isolated behavior;
- quote-mining;
- domain overgeneralization;
- romanticizing adversity.

## Core distinction

Success story ≠ causal evidence.

Behavioral example ≠ universal prescription.

Reputation ≠ evidence.

Outcome ≠ proof of method.

## Working synthesis

Behavior → Context → Mechanism → Evidence → Alternative Explanations → Boundary → Transfer

This is a book synthesis, not an academic taxonomy.

## Evidence families to support future case selection

- causal inference and confounding;
- survivorship and selection effects;
- halo effect and attribution;
- learning transfer and boundary conditions;
- hindsight and retrospective reconstruction.

## Editorial rule

Prefer documented behavior over inspirational quotes. Prefer comparative cases where possible. If evidence is insufficient, mark the claim unknown rather than filling the gap with narrative.

## Release condition

Before adding named contemporary figures or controversial historical figures, run a source audit and preserve attribution for disputed claims.

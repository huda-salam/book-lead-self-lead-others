# Evidence Update — Chapter 40: Cara Membaca Sebuah Kasus

## Evidence focus

Chapter 40 establishes a reusable method for analyzing leadership and organizational cases without jumping from a short narrative to a character judgment or single-cause explanation.

### Core evidence posture

- A case narrative is a selective representation of reality, not reality itself.
- Good case reasoning separates observations, interpretations, assumptions, missing information, competing explanations, decisions, actions, outcomes, and learning.
- Hindsight bias can make outcomes look more predictable than they were from the information available at the time.
- Attribution should consider person, task, context, system, incentives, information, authority, and interaction rather than defaulting to character explanations.
- Counterfactual reasoning can clarify causal assumptions but remains dependent on evidence and assumptions.
- Different stakeholders can have different legitimate objectives and information.
- A useful case analysis distinguishes what was known then from what is known now.
- Recommendations should follow diagnosis; multiple plausible options may remain.
- Cases are useful for transfer only when the reader identifies mechanisms and boundary conditions rather than copying surface behavior.

## Editorial implications

1. Read the case twice: first for what happened, then for what is uncertain.
2. Build a timeline.
3. Separate observation from interpretation.
4. Identify actors, interests, authority, information, constraints, and incentives.
5. Identify decision points and available alternatives.
6. Reconstruct what information was available at the time.
7. Test causal explanations against alternatives.
8. Separate decision quality from outcome quality.
9. Identify what could have been done differently without assuming hindsight certainty.
10. Extract transferable principles plus boundary conditions.

## Proposed book synthesis

**Case → Timeline → Actors → Facts → Unknowns → Decision Points → Options → Mechanisms → Consequences → Attribution → Alternatives → Learning/Transfer**

This is a book working model, not a validated case-analysis taxonomy.
